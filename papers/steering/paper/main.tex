\begin{abstract}
Autonomous agents that tend a shared substrate can measure it while it
runs: couplings are discovered, response curves carry error bars, and
the curves compose across hops. This paper asks what such a map is
worth when the world is not still. The answer is the \emph{steerwish}
--- intent made keepable: a promise to a live system, declared by its
holder, kept against drift, and refused --- not faked --- when the
measured range says no. Four claims, each carried by receipts
from a live measurement campaign. \emph{The map can steer}: through a
four-line promise grammar, composed predictions land readings inside
promised bands, repeating across seed runs --- and the boundary is
stated as measured, a limit of map density rather than of principle.
\emph{Refusal}: a target outside the measured range is declined by
name, with zero actuation. \emph{Drift survival}: a declared drift
event is survived end to end --- misses stamp, the map re-opens, the
reading re-lands --- with stable numbers across runs. \emph{Upkeep
economics}: a three-flight envelope prices the keeping in rounds and
waterings, read absolutely between what luck alone keeps and what
perfect hindsight could have kept, with the envelope's two failures
reported as the honest edges of the economics. Keeping is
priced in rounds and waterings, never against external keepers: the
comparison is a named follow-up.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} autonomous agents, multi-agent systems,
steerwish, promises, refusal, drift, maintenance cost, model-based
control, system identification, self-adaptive systems, live systems

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

When autonomous agents tend the same substrate, each one's parameter
changes travel through the substrate into the others' readings. Prior
work established that this hidden coupling is measurable without
central observers and without stopping the system: edges are
discovered by guardrail-bounded probing between the agents
themselves~\cite{p1}, each edge yields a response curve with per-point
error bars~\cite{p2}, and measured curves compose across nonlinear
hops and converging paths~\cite{p3}. The map that results is a
certified, priced model of a live world.

This paper asks the question that line of work ends at: can the
measured map --- the substrate's \emph{connectome}: every coupling
edge with its measured response curve --- \emph{do} anything?
Specifically --- can a promise be held about the
far end of the substrate, through the map, while the substrate moves?
Holding a promise is not an optimum-seeking problem. It is a keeping
problem: the reading must stay inside a band, the world drifts, and
the keeping is work. Work has a price. And some promises, measured
honestly, cannot be kept at all --- the keeper that cannot see far
enough must say so and touch nothing.

The claims, in the order the campaign receipts them:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The map can steer.} Invert once through the composed
map, act once, land inside the promised band; repeats across seeds
show it is not luck. The boundary is stated as measured, not assumed
away (\S\ref{sec:steering}).
\item \textbf{Refusal.} A target outside the measured range is
refused by name, with zero actuation --- knowing what you do not
know, under action (\S\ref{sec:refusal}).
\item \textbf{Drift survival.} The promise survives a declared drift
event: misses stamp, the map re-opens, the reading re-lands, with
stable numbers across runs (\S\ref{sec:drift}).
\item \textbf{Upkeep economics.} Rounds per kept interval and the
cost of watering, read absolutely between what nothing tends to keep
(luck) and what an omniscient tender would have kept (hindsight),
with the envelope's failures reported as its
edges (\S\ref{sec:upkeep}).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{What flew is the smallest grammar that exercises the whole
loop.} Two claim lines and two term lines, one dial, a two-stage
path: a proof of concept, deliberately simple. The claims of this
paper are about the loop's existence and its honesty --- that a
promise can be steered, refused, survived, and priced --- not about
its reach. Scaling is the open direction the boundaries point at:
denser maps, deeper corridors, more lines, and eventually several
promises sharing one substrate.

One containment rules everything below. The comparison is contained:
no external keeper family appears, and the honesty of every number is carried by two internal anchors:
what nothing tends to keep (the same promise, nobody tending --- is
the band satisfied by luck?) and what an omniscient tender would
have kept (computed after the fact from the recorded truth). Every
kept fraction in this paper reads
absolutely between those anchors. A
comparison across keeper families is the named follow-up's spine,
not a row here; the pricing throughout stays in the promise's own
units --- rounds and waterings.

\section{The world and the grammar}
\label{sec:grammar}

\emph{Substrate.} The substrate is whatever the agents tend:
physical, computational, or mathematical. In this campaign it is a
live bench of coupled nodes; each node exposes parameters (dials) and
readings (objectives), and a change of one node's dial travels
through measured couplings into other nodes' readings. A truth
channel records the bench's own state throughout --- the answer key.
It is never read at decision time; it scores after the fact.

\emph{The connectome, and the map.} The substrate's coupling
structure, once measured edge by edge --- each edge with its
response curve and error bar, the work of the prior papers --- is
its \emph{connectome}. From here on, this paper calls the connectome
simply \emph{the map}: the thing that is planned through, refused
for, and paid for (Figure~\ref{fig:connectome}).

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{figures/connectome}
\caption{The connectome and the corridor. Nodes of the substrate,
coupling edges measured (curve and error bar, prior work). A
promise steers through the connectome --- here one dial, two
stages, to the promised reading.}
\label{fig:connectome}
\end{figure}

\emph{Agents.} The agents are tending agents, never optimizers: they
tend a shared substrate. A keeper holds a promise; scorers witness
lines; measurers refresh the map. None of them maximizes anything.

\emph{Promise.} A promise is a band $[\mathrm{lo},\mathrm{hi}]$ on a
reading, held over time: the delivered reading must sit inside the
band for the promise to count as kept in a given interval. Promises
conjoin: several lines must hold simultaneously, and the conjunction
is exact --- one line out ends the flight's landing claim.

\emph{The four-line grammar.} The campaign's shape is $2{+}2$: two
claim lines (bands on two readings, one of them two hops away) and
two term lines (bands on related readings that the promise's
semantics require to hold). Every line --- claim or term --- is
judged by the same per-line verdict record, with the same bars.

\emph{Naming, once.} Internally, the held promise of this paper is a
\emph{steerwish} --- intent made keepable: a promise \emph{to} a live
system, declared by its holder and never invented by the engine, kept
against drift, and refused --- not faked --- when the system says no.
Its family contains one sibling, the \emph{seekwish} (an exploration
wish, which deliberately explores the substrate's range rather than
holding a band); it is named here for completeness and claimed
nowhere in this paper.

\emph{Rounds and waterings.} A \emph{round} is one full pass of the
keeping loop: plan through the map, act, judge. A \emph{watering} is
a discrete corrective intervention, fired when the reading leaves the
band by more than continuous compensation can absorb. The loop
itself is Figure~\ref{fig:loop}.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{figures/loop}
\caption{The keeping loop. A promise holds a band on a reading of a
shared substrate. The keeper plans through its measured map, acts
once, and the per-line verdict records judge the delivered reading

watering fires when the world exceeds what compensation absorbs.}
\label{fig:loop}
\end{figure}

The engine under the loop is prior work, used as settled: response
curves with per-point bars~\cite{p2} and their composition across
hops~\cite{p3} --- together, the connectome of the preceding
paragraph. Neither prior result is re-litigated here; what this
paper asks is what the map is worth once a promise must be held on
it while the substrate drifts --- steering by it, refusing for it,
surviving on it, and paying for it.

\section{What makes a verdict countable}
\label{sec:instrument}

Every claim in this paper rests on verdicts --- landed, missed,
refused --- so the rules that make a verdict countable are part of
the instrument. There are two.

\emph{Everything is recorded.} Every run writes the same diary ---
per tick (reading, in band or not, dial position, trial spent) and
per event (declared, planned, missed, probed, re-planned, landed,
broken, released). The truth channel --- the bench's own answer key
--- records the real state alongside. It is never read at decision
time; it scores after the fact. Verdicts are recomputed from these
recordings, never taken from a run's badge or summary.

\emph{A verdict needs its witness.} Every promise line has its own
verdict record, and the record only counts readings taken while a
scorer was assigned to that line. The rule is simple: no assignment,
no verdict --- an unwitnessed line is not a passed line. And a plan
that moves dials must carry its receivers, so that every line the
promise depends on stays witnessed while the plan executes.

That is the whole section: record everything, and count only what
was witnessed.

Every number in this paper reads three ways: if nothing tended,
what we tended, and if something omniscient had tended. The first
is the floor --- the same promise, the same drifting world, nobody
acting: whatever stays inside the band is what luck buys. If the
floor is high, the band was vacuous and the run certifies nothing;
that verdict is itself a receipt. The third is the ceiling ---
computed after the run from the recorded answer key: what perfect
hindsight would have kept. No oracle ever flew live; it is a
calculation, not a competitor. Between the two sits what we tended
--- the keeper's number, readable on its own.

Doctrine, inherited: the sweep is characterization, not pass/fail.
Expected degradation is the floor and is reported; unexpected failure
is an instrument fault and is cured with receipts (the witnessing
repairs of \S\ref{sec:instrument} are exactly that class). Boundary
rows are receipts, never discards.

\subsection{Steering: the four-line grammar}
\label{sec:steering}

The four-line conjunction --- two \emph{claim} lines (readings the
holder asked about) and two \emph{term} lines (supporting readings
that must also hold) --- flew through
one dial and a two-stage path.

\emph{The landing receipts.} Both term lines judged \emph{and
honored live} --- $0.8204\,[0.64,1.00]$ and $0.3534\,[0.16,0.52]$;
one claim line judged missed by $0.022$ (predicted $0.6486$,
delivered $0.5685$), stated as its row says. The follow-up
hold-shape flight landed with its claims judged
(Table~\ref{tab:s1}).

\emph{The boundary, as measured.} At the campaign's map density the
best claim miss is $0.022$ against bars near $0.01$, and the joint
down-shape ($-0.10/-0.06$) was refused by name three times across
fresh runs: on coarse maps the composed plan
reads conflicts that are not real, and the planner refuses what it
cannot compose honestly. This is a boundary of map density --- a
denser map composes the two-stage path honestly, and the four-of-four
landing is one dense map away. Boundary, not failure
--- and the boundary's direction is known.

\emph{Repetition.} Five seed runs, four landed (a, c, d, e;
Table~\ref{tab:s2}): margins $0.047$--$0.063$ above band floors,
two flights including honest undecidable first windows (bars
straddling an edge at $n{=}3$) before their landing stamps,
declare-to-verdict spans of 14--60\,s. The fifth (S2-b) missed by
$0.004$ over the edge --- declared four hours after its
pre-measurement finished, a timing artifact of the operators, not
the engine; the run sat and wandered, and the row says so. The
repetition bar (three of five) is cleared (Table~\ref{tab:s2}).

The instrument's own trail is part of the receipt. The first attempt
in this run also refused --- \emph{unmeasured outcome} --- and the

This is the epistemic half of the loop: infinity versus zero. The
keeper does not extrapolate beyond its measured range --- it states
what would be required ($0.6363$), what is available ($0.1184$), and
touches nothing.

\subsection{Drift survival: the arc with stable numbers}
\label{sec:drift}

The battery (five runs, one declared shape change each) repeats one arc
with stable numbers: slide to conviction 76--90\,s; misses to streak
two, plus one undecidable; conviction ratios $65$--$300\times$ the
plan's own error bars; declare to landed 1--2\,s.

One run, told fully. The world moves off the finished map. The
standing watch fires unprompted --- 19\,s cue-to-alarm, reading
$0.0618$ past a $0.0500$ band, a second sample at $0.1557$ --- and
the targeted probe returns the first usable hint in the instrument's
history, 76\,s after the slide cue. The measurement re-opens around
the moved region; the refresh is physical (the probing cycle is
visible on the strip); the reading re-lands; unattended regions
heal. The conviction itself is priced: predicted shifts with their
bars against delivered readings land $65$--$300\times$ the plan's
own bars --- far enough out that drift, not noise, is the verdict.

\subsection{Upkeep: what keeping costs}
\label{sec:upkeep}

The message of this section: holding a promise on a moving world
costs work, and the cost can be measured. One number carries it.
Over four hours of a slowly sliding world, the keeper kept the
reading inside its band for $0.874$ of the judged windows --- doing
nothing would have kept $0.013$, perfect foresight $1.000$
(Table~\ref{tab:d2}, Figure~\ref{fig:bracket}). Figure~\ref{fig:hold}
shows the recorded hold itself: every judged window of the four
hours.

The price behind the number is continuous work: the keeper served
trials for the whole four hours, compensating as the world slid. It
never lost the band outright --- zero misses; the windows it did not
keep were minutes spent riding the band's edge while the world
wandered. The promise's own claim line judged landed throughout,
median reading $0.98$. A \emph{watering} --- a discrete corrective
move --- never became necessary: compensation never fell that far
behind.

Two earlier flights taught how to read the number. In the first,
the band was too wide ($\pm 0.15$ against a $0.15$ slide): doing
nothing also kept everything ($1.000$), so the keeper's $0.993$
meant nothing --- a satisfied luck floor certifies nothing. In the
second, the band was narrow but placed on a fleeting reading (an
operator error, kept on the record): the world outran the
compensation --- sixteen breaks, kept only $0.093$. Those two
failures are the edges of the economics; the third flight is the
keep between them.

Why is perfect foresight exactly $1.000$? With the slide known in
advance, every window can be compensated before it happens ---
foresight is never surprised. The keeper's gap to it ($0.126$) is
the measured price of acting on a map instead of knowing the
future.

\emph{Why hindsight is exactly $1.000$, and what the gap means.}
With the drift path known in advance, every window can be
pre-compensated; perfect foresight is never surprised, so it keeps
all windows --- the ceiling is a structural $1.000$, not an
achievement. The keeper's gap to it ($0.126$) is the measured
price of acting on a map instead of a crystal ball: the keeper
never broke --- zero misses --- but it rides the edge while the
world wanders, and edge-riding costs windows. For contrast, v2's
gap was $0.907$: there the world outpaced compensation, and that is
what a bad gap looks like. The oracle is a ceiling by construction,
not an arm that flew; it bounds what any keeper could have done
with the truth in hand.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{What the map is worth under drift.} The worth of a maintained
map is read absolutely: between a floor that says what luck buys
($0.013$) and a ceiling that says what omniscience buys ($1.000$),
the keeper holds $0.874$ for four hours through a saturating slide.
The renewal receipt certifies the \emph{maintained} property live
against ground truth --- map error jumps at the break and collapses
at the refresh --- and it costs nothing extra: it is a byproduct of
runs the campaign already flew.

\emph{Why not just optimize, one paragraph.} A maximizer and a
keeper share machinery --- both plan through a model --- but their
objects differ. Pointed at a substrate, a maximizer drives to an
optimum and structurally cannot comply with a promise whose band
sits away from it: a promise is a chosen state, not an optimum.
The nearest ancestor of this paper's refusal is safe model-based
optimization with Gaussian processes --- the safe-optimization line
bounds exploration to a trusted region --- but its stance is more
permissive than the one receipted here: those keepers may act inside
the trusted set, while this keeper declined the target itself, by
name, with zero actuation. Adaptive model-predictive control
re-learns moving plants toward references; it does not price upkeep
in the plant's own units or refuse the unmeasurable as a first-class
verdict.

\emph{The boundaries, collected.} The four-line landing is gated on
map density (best claim miss $0.022$ vs bars ${\approx}0.01$; three
named refusals of the joint down-shape); the upkeep economics
have two measured edges (a vacuous band, a wrong cut) and one kept
middle; full grid re-learn outlives the run window at walking pace;
the second lap rests on a single receipt. And what flew is the
smallest grammar that exercises the whole loop --- scaling (denser
maps, deeper corridors, more lines, simultaneous promises) is the
named direction. A journal-only literature
residual sits outside arXiv's reach --- noted in the writing-day
sweep, not blocking.

\emph{What this paper deliberately does not contain.} No external
keeper, no comparative language, no scoreboard. The honesty is
carried by the free bracket and by rows that report their own
failures. And the pricing stays in the promise's own units ---
rounds and waterings --- by design.

\subsection*{Future work}

\emph{Scaling.} What flew is the smallest grammar that exercises
the whole loop. The open directions are the ones the boundaries
point at: denser maps, deeper corridors, more lines, and
eventually several promises sharing one substrate.

\emph{The comparison.} This paper reads every number between
internal anchors --- luck and foresight. The natural next question
is how the same loop fares against external keeper families on the
same runs. That comparison is the follow-up paper's question, and
it stands on these receipts.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Related work is context, not a wall; the phrasing below is
deliberately defensive. Each line holds a piece of what this paper
assembles --- no single one holds the assembly.

\emph{Careful optimizers.} A family of methods explores a system
cautiously: they only try settings their internal model already
considers safe, and expand slowly. Representative work optimizes
quadrotor controllers through Gaussian-process models~\cite{berkenkamp2015},
proves probably-safe variants of that
idea~\cite{tokmak2024}, brings trust-region safety to automotive
control~\cite{menn2025}, and adds safety to a classic evolutionary
optimizer~\cite{uchida2024}. All of them bound \emph{exploration} ---
where they may poke next. The refusal receipted here is the stricter
stance: the target itself is declined, by name, with zero actuation,
and the one declining owns actuators.

\emph{Controllers that re-learn.} Adaptive model-predictive
control~\cite{tanaskovic2017} and its modern stochastic
variants~\cite{knaup2024} keep their model of a changing plant up to
date and steer toward a setpoint. They lack the promise grammar ---
bands as first-class objects, conjunctions, a named refusal of the
unmeasurable --- and they do not price the upkeep.

\emph{The economics of promising.} Work on commitment devices in
cooperative AI~\cite{sun2023} studies when agents should bind
themselves to promises, in terms of norms and incentives. This paper
prices the keeping --- against a substrate, with measurements.

\emph{The cost of keeping systems alive.} Self-adaptive
software~\cite{abufouda2014,abufouda2014b} and the recent mapping of
agentic technical debt --- the accumulating cost of keeping AI-based
systems viable~\cite{tukur2026} --- name these costs. None measure
them against a luck floor and a hindsight ceiling.

\emph{Systems that decline to answer.} Machine learning has a reject
option: classifiers and regressors that say ``not me'' when
unsure~\cite{franc2021,franc2025,noskov2023}, and safe exploration
that declines unsafe states~\cite{roderick2020}. All decline
\emph{answers}. Here the one declining owns actuators and stops the
machinery --- an action declined, with the reason named and the
anatomy priced.

To our knowledge, no neighbor does what this loop does together:
keep a declared promise on a live, drifting substrate, refuse by
name what it cannot measure, and price the keeping between luck and
perfect hindsight. We are not aware of anyone else this close.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Four kept promises. The map steers: the same scenario landed again
and again, and the one boundary is a map-density limit with a known
cure. The map refuses: asked for a target beyond its measured range,
it declined by name and touched nothing. The map survives drift:
the world moved, the misses were counted, the map was refreshed, the
reading returned. And the keeping is priced: between what luck keeps
and what foresight would have kept, the keeper held most of it, for
four hours, without a single break. A held promise
on a drifting substrate is a measurable, refusable, payable thing.
What flew is the smallest grammar that exercises the whole loop;
scaling is the named direction, and the comparison across keeper
families is the follow-up's question --- both stand on these
receipts.

\section*{Reproducibility}

The campaign flew on a self-hosted live bench --- the estate's
generic mathematical bench, the same bench family the prior papers
measured. Its logic is simple: nodes expose parameters (dials) and
return readings; planted couplings --- chosen shapes, such as
saturating or tent maps, at chosen strengths --- carry one node's
dial changes into another node's reading; a drift schedule moves the
planted values over the run's life; and a closed-form truth channel
records what was actually planted at every tick. Nothing planted is
visible to the keeper: it learns the couplings by probing, exactly
as in the prior papers; the truth channel exists only to score. The
run shapes of Table~\ref{tab:shape} ---
promise, band, scheduled drift, window --- are then the full
scenario definitions.

Each run's record is retained
in the evidence bundles --- the per-tick diary, the per-line verdict
cards, the reading strips, the truth and drift recordings --- except
where cards did not survive collection; those runs are carried by
their documented numbers, marked as such in the figures and tables. Every
figure regenerates from its bundle by the committed scripts, and the
upkeep bracket --- floor, kept fraction, oracle ceiling --- recomputes
from the strip log alone; verdicts are recomputed from curves and
strips, never read from run badges. Full independent reproduction
requires this estate's stack; on a compatible bench,
Table~\ref{tab:shape} plus the bundles are everything a re-run needs.
