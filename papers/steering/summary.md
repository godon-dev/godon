## Sealed claims

1. **The map can steer** (S1+S2) — invert once through the composed map, act
   once, land within the promised bars; repeats across seeds prove it is not
   luck.
2. **What the map saves** (S3) — map-ful (one plan, one move, a few reads) vs
   map-less (blind family at its best, median/quartiles probes-to-tolerance).
   Geography of savings: the map saves most where guessing is hardest.
3. **Refusal** (S4) — target outside the measured range → refuse, never
   extrapolate. Infinity vs zero: knowing what you don't know, under action.
4. **Drift survival** (D1) — the wish survives a declared drift event: miss
   stamps, re-walk, re-land.
5. **Upkeep economics** (D2) — rounds per kept interval and the cost of one
   watering; payback ceiling derived from the wish's own quiet (no magic
   constants), refusal-with-reason at the wall.

## Baseline discipline (naming rulings are binding)

- Blind family at its best: constrained TPE, CMA-ES, NSGA-II (+ random/grid
  controls). Do-nothing and oracle arms bracket the possible range, so every
  arm's position reads absolutely.
- In text: "sequential model-based optimization (Optuna's TPE sampler)" —
  NEVER "Bayesian optimization". Our agents are autonomous/tending agents —
  NEVER "optimizers". Substrate = physical, computational, or mathematical.
- The doctrinal delta scenario: a band OFFSET FROM THE OPTIMUM. The blind
  optimizer structurally cannot comply — it maximizes away from the band.
  A wish is a chosen state, not a maximum.
- Boundaries of THIS paper: composition is P3's settled claim (used as the
  engine, not re-litigated); the handler-contract totality theorem is P5's
  (reserved — a theorem, not an empirical cell); the MBRL ablation row is a
  named follow-up decision at writing time (sealed spine does not grow).
